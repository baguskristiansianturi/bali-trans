What to confirm before booking a Bali experience

A simple checklist to help you compare options and avoid surprises before you make a plan.

Photos can make an experience look straightforward, but the practical details matter just as much. Before you choose an activity in Bali, take a moment to understand what the operator is offering, what you need to arrange yourself and which terms apply to your group.

1. Know what is included

Read the description carefully and ask what the price covers. Depending on the activity, useful questions may include whether equipment, a guide, refreshments, entrance fees, parking or transport are included. Do not assume an optional add-on is part of the base price.

2. Confirm timing and meeting point

Check the start time, expected duration, exact meeting point and how early you should arrive. If transport is offered, confirm the pick-up location, waiting arrangements and whether return transport is included. Leave realistic time for traffic and travel between separate stops.

3. Ask about weather and changes

Outdoor and sea-based activities can depend on local conditions. Ask who makes the decision if conditions change, whether the activity may be delayed or cancelled, and what alternatives or refunds are available. For safety-critical decisions, follow the operator and relevant local authorities rather than relying on an old article.

4. Understand price and cancellation

Before paying, confirm the total amount, currency, payment timing, cancellation deadline, refund method and any fees. Keep the written confirmation and the provider’s contact details. If the terms are unclear, ask for clarification before committing.

5. Keep a final checklist

  • Names and number of participants are correct.
  • Age, mobility or equipment requirements have been checked.
  • Meeting point, start time and duration are clear.
  • Inclusions, exclusions, total price and currency are understood.
  • Weather, cancellation and contact arrangements are written down.

A little preparation helps you compare options on more than appearance alone. If an important detail is missing, treat it as a question to resolve—not as a promise.
